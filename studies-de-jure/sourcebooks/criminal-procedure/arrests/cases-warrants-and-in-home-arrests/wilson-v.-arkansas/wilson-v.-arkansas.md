# Wilson v. Arkansas

## Wilson v. Arkansas (1995)

Context: At time of the Constitution's framing, common law instructed LE to announce presence and authority before entering a home to make an arrest - the "knock and announce" principle

#### **Investigation and Controlled Buys**

* In **November and December 1992**, Sharlene Wilson became the target of a narcotics investigation conducted by the Arkansas State Police.
* A **confidential informant**, acting under police direction, made **multiple controlled purchases** of illegal drugs from Wilson:
  * The informant bought **marijuana and methamphetamine** at Wilson’s home, which she shared with **Bryson Jacobs**.

#### **Escalation and Threatening Conduct**

* On **December 30**, the informant arranged another drug purchase via telephone.
* The meeting took place at a **local store** rather than the home.
* During this encounter:
  * Wilson **produced a semiautomatic pistol**.
  * She **threatened to kill the informant** if she turned out to be cooperating with police.
  * Despite the threat, Wilson completed the sale of marijuana.

#### **Warrants and Supporting Information**

* Based on these events, police obtained:
  * A **search warrant** for Wilson’s home
  * **Arrest warrants** for both Wilson and Jacobs
* Supporting affidavits included:
  * Details of the **controlled drug transactions**
  * Information that **Jacobs had prior convictions** for **arson and firebombing**, suggesting potential danger

#### **Execution of the Search**

* The search was executed **the next day (December 31, 1992)**.
* Officers encountered:
  * The **main door already open**
  * A **closed but unlocked screen door**
* Officers:
  * **Opened the screen door**
  * **Entered while announcing** their identity and that they had a warrant
  * Critically, they **did not knock before entering**

#### **Evidence Seized**

Inside the home, officers discovered:

* **Marijuana, methamphetamine, and valium**
* **Drug paraphernalia**
* A **firearm and ammunition**
* Wilson herself in the bathroom **attempting to destroy evidence by flushing marijuana**

#### **Charges and Conviction**

Wilson and Jacobs were charged with:

* Delivery of marijuana
* Delivery of methamphetamine
* Possession of drug paraphernalia
* Possession of marijuana

Wilson was convicted and sentenced to **32 years in prison**.

**I:** Whether the knock and announce principle is relevant to the Fourth Amendment reasonableness test

H: Yes

**Rule:** Common law knock and announce principle is part of the test for reasonableness under the 4th Amend; depending on the circumstances, unannounced entry can be unreasonable

### **Reasoning**

#### **1. Historical/Common-Law Foundation**

* The Court, per Justice Thomas, emphasized that Fourth Amendment interpretation is informed by **common-law principles at the time of the Founding**.
* At common law:
  * Officers could **break into a dwelling** to execute warrants.
  * But they generally had to **announce their presence and purpose first**.
* This principle was widely recognized by authorities such as:
  * **Sir Matthew Hale**
  * **William Hawkins**
  * **Sir William Blackstone**

#### **2. Incorporation into the Fourth Amendment**

* The Court concluded that:
  * The **method of entry** into a home is part of what makes a search “reasonable.”
  * Therefore, the knock-and-announce principle is **embedded in the Fourth Amendment**.

#### **3. Flexibility of the Rule**

* The Court rejected a **categorical requirement** of announcement.
* Instead, it adopted a **balancing approach**:
  * Announcement is generally required
  * But exceptions exist when law enforcement interests outweigh it

#### **4. Recognized Exceptions**

The Court identified several historically grounded exceptions:

* **Threat of violence** to officers
* **Hot pursuit** of a fleeing suspect
* **Risk of destruction of evidence**

#### **5. Application to Wilson**

* The Court did **not decide whether the officers’ conduct here was reasonable**.
* Instead, it noted:
  * Wilson had threatened an informant with a gun
  * Evidence (drugs) was easily destructible
* These facts **could justify a no-knock entry**, but:
  * The Arkansas Supreme Court had not analyzed this issue under the correct constitutional framework

**But**, LE has **countervailing interests (e.g.):**

**threat of physical harm to police**

**the fact that an officer is pursuing a recently escaped arrestee**

**the existence of reason to believe that evidence would likely be destroyed if advance notice were given**

**Here, the police may have reasonably believed that knock and announce would have placed them in peril and would have produced an unreasonable risk that Wilson would easily destroy disposable narcotics evidence**

(Remanded back to state court for reasonableness determinations)
